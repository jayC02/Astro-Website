EPC changes in Scotland from 2028

Scotland is replacing the EPC with a new certificate that has three headline ratings and lasts five years. The start date has moved to 30 April 2028. Here is what changes, what stays the same, and what to do now.

Updated 18 November 2026 by the Scotland EPC team at Scottish Energy Services

The short version

  • Today's EPCs, with one A to G rating and 10-year validity, carry on until 30 April 2028.
  • From 30 April 2028, new EPCs will have three headline ratings: Heat Retention, Heating System and Energy Cost.
  • New-style EPCs will be valid for five years, not ten, and come with a property report instead of a recommendations report.
  • An old-style EPC can still be used to sell or let during a one-year transition to 30 April 2029.
  • A minimum standard for private lets, based on the new Heat Retention Rating, has been proposed but not yet confirmed.

Key dates

  1. TodayIn force

    Current EPCs

    RdSAP survey, one A to G energy efficiency rating, valid for 10 years. Needed before you market a home for sale or let.

  2. 31 October 2026In force

    Register fee rise

    The fee for lodging an EPC on the Scottish EPC Register went up to fund the new digital register and methodology.

  3. 30 April 2028Confirmed

    New-style EPCs only

    Three headline ratings: Heat Retention, Heating System and Energy Cost. New EPCs are valid for five years and come with a property report.

  4. 30 April 2028 to 30 April 2029Confirmed

    One-year transition

    An old-style EPC under 10 years old can still be used to sell or let. If the property is sold or let again in that year, a new-style EPC is needed.

  5. From 30 April 2029Confirmed

    Old EPCs retired

    Only new-style EPCs can be used for a sale or a new let.

  6. Not yet setProposed

    Minimum standard for private lets

    Draft plans would require private rented homes to reach Heat Retention Rating band C, first at a change of tenancy and later for all lets. The timetable has not been confirmed.

Why the date moved

The reform was due on 31 October 2026. In August 2026 the Scottish Government laid amending regulations moving the start to 30 April 2028. The new EPCs depend on the UK Government's Home Energy Model, which replaces the current SAP and RdSAP calculation method, and on a new digital EPC Register. Both were delayed across the UK, so Scotland aligned its timetable. The policy itself has not changed.

The three new ratings

  • Heat Retention Rating

    How well the building holds on to heat, from A to G, based on the fabric: walls, roof, floor, windows and draughts. It ignores the heating system.

  • Heating System Rating

    How good the main heating system is, taking account of its efficiency, emissions and running costs.

  • Energy Cost Rating

    The modelled cost of running the home, closest to today's headline rating, which is also cost based.

The new certificate will also show carbon dioxide emissions. Splitting the rating means a well-insulated home with old heating, or a poorly insulated home with a heat pump, will no longer be hidden behind one blended number.

Five-year validity

10 yearsold-style EPCs issued before 30 April 2028
5 yearsnew-style EPCs
2×roughly how often landlords will renew

If you let a property, plan for a new EPC roughly twice as often as now. Old-style EPCs issued before 30 April 2028 keep their 10-year validity, but after 30 April 2029 only a new-style EPC can be used for a sale or a new let.

The transition, step by step

  1. Before 30 April 2028

    Nothing changes. Assessors issue current-style EPCs, valid for 10 years.

  2. From 30 April 2028

    Any new EPC must be new style. You can still use a valid old-style EPC for a sale or let.

  3. 30 April 2028 to 30 April 2029

    If a property marketed on an old-style EPC is sold or let again within this year, a new-style EPC and property report will be needed.

  4. From 30 April 2029

    Only new-style EPCs can be used for sales and new lets.

The proposed standard for private lets

The Scottish Government has consulted on draft regulations that would require private rented homes to reach Heat Retention Rating band C. The consultation proposed applying it first to new tenancies from 2028 and to all private lets by 2033, with a cost cap and exemptions. Because the rating cannot be produced until new-style EPCs exist, the timetable is likely to move with the EPC reform and has not been confirmed.

The Scottish Government has said it intends Heat Retention band C to be broadly equivalent to today's EPC band C, but it measures something different. A home that is C today because of a cheap-to-run heating system could fall short on heat retention, and the reverse.

What to do now

If you are letting in the next 18 months

Get a current-style EPC as normal. It stays valid for 10 years and can be used through the transition. Read the recommendations report: insulation work you do now will count towards Heat Retention later.

If you are selling

Your Home Report will include an EPC as now. Buyers will increasingly look at insulation, so evidence of loft, wall and window upgrades is worth keeping.

If you own an older home

Fabric improvements such as loft insulation, cavity wall insulation, draught-proofing and better glazing will matter most under the Heat Retention Rating. Your current EPC recommendations report is a good starting list.
