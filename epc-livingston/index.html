Homes in Livingston

Livingston was designated a new town in 1962, so most of its housing dates from the 1960s onwards:

  • 1960s and 1970s new town housing, including terraces and low-rise flats, some of non-traditional construction.
  • Timber-frame houses from the 1980s onwards, very common in West Lothian, usually with insulation built into the walls.
  • Newer estates around the town, typically rating B or C.
  • Older stone homes in Linlithgow and the villages, with solid walls and more limited options.

EPCs start from £70 for a flat and £85 for a house, with £120 for an EPC and legionella risk assessment together, all plus VAT.

What happens on the visit

An accredited assessor surveys every room, the boiler, the hot water and the loft if it can be reached safely, taking photos and measurements. Most visits take under an hour. Your EPC is lodged on the Scottish EPC Register and emailed to you, normally within 24 hours.

Questions

Do you cover the rest of West Lothian?

Yes, including Bathgate, Linlithgow, Broxburn and the villages, and the whole of Scotland.

Can you tell if my house is timber frame?

Usually, from the wall thickness, the construction date and what we can see. Building warrant drawings or a Home Report help if you have them.
