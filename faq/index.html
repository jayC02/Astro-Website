Questions and answers

Straight answers to the questions landlords, agents, sellers and homeowners ask us most.

About EPCs

How long does an EPC last in Scotland?

Ten years for current EPCs. From 30 April 2028, new-style EPCs will be valid for five years. Existing EPCs can still be used during a one-year transition to 30 April 2029.

What is changing with EPCs in 2028?

New-style EPCs will show three headline ratings, for heat retention, the heating system and energy cost, instead of one. They will be valid for five years and come with a property report. See our 2028 guide.

Do I need an EPC to sell my home in Scotland?

Yes. For most sales the EPC is part of the Home Report. If your sale is exempt from the Home Report, you still need an EPC before marketing the home.

Where does the EPC have to be displayed?

In Scotland the EPC must be fixed somewhere in the property, usually in the meter cupboard or next to the boiler. The rating must also appear in adverts for a sale or let.

What happens if I market a property without an EPC?

The council can issue a penalty charge notice of £500 for a home, and buyers and tenants are entitled to see one before they commit.

How can I check if a property already has an EPC?

Search the address on the Scottish EPC Register. It shows the rating, the date and when it expires.

Can I improve my rating before the survey?

Yes. Fit low-energy bulbs, make sure the heating controls are working, and gather paperwork for insulation, windows, the boiler and any solar panels. Evidence lets the assessor record what is really there instead of using defaults for the age of the home.

About legionella

Do landlords in Scotland need a legionella risk assessment?

Yes. The Repairing Standard guidance says the water system must be assessed before the property is first let and the assessment reviewed at intervals of no more than two years.

Is there a legionella certificate?

No. The requirement is a risk assessment. Water testing is not normally needed in a home.

What temperatures should my water be?

Stored hot water at 60°C, hot water at the taps at least 50°C within a minute, and cold water below 20°C within two minutes. Legionella can multiply between 20°C and 45°C.

Do short-term lets need one?

Yes. Short-term lets must meet the Repairing Standard, which includes the duty to assess legionella risk.

On the day

Do I need to be there?

No, as long as someone can let us in. Tenants and letting agents often arrange access.

What do you need to see?

Every room, the boiler and heating controls, the hot water cylinder if there is one, the loft hatch if it can be reached safely, and the taps and showers if you have booked a legionella assessment.

How long does the visit take?

Most visits take under an hour. Larger houses, and EPC and legionella together, take a little longer.
