Glasgow water systems

Most Glasgow rented flats have one of two systems, and the risk is quite different:

  • Combi boilers heat water on demand with nothing stored, so they are usually low risk. We still check outlet temperatures, showers and any little-used taps.
  • Hot water cylinders, often with an electric immersion heater in older tenements and 1960s to 1970s flats. Here we check the cylinder reaches 60°C and the hot water at the taps reaches 50°C. Top-floor flats sometimes have a cold water tank in the loft above.

Houses in the suburbs are more likely to have a loft tank and cylinder, which is where we most often find a missing lid, debris or warm cold water.

Empty flats and student lets

Water that sits still is the biggest risk. Flats that stand empty between tenancies, and student lets over the summer, should have every outlet flushed before the next tenant moves in. We include this in the action list if it applies.

What we check

We identify the water system, measure temperatures at the taps, cylinder and any tank, inspect tanks for lids and debris, and look for showers, dead legs and little-used outlets. You get a written assessment with the temperatures recorded, an overall risk level and a short list of actions. More about legionella risk assessments.

The rules in Scotland

The Repairing Standard guidance says a private landlord must have the water system assessed for legionella before the property is first let, and review it at intervals of no more than two years. There is no legionella certificate and water testing is not normally needed.
